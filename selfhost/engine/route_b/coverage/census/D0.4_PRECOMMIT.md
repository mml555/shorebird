# D0.4 precommit — replication corpus, chosen before any of its numbers exist

**Written and committed before a single census row was produced for any
candidate below.** D0.4 is replication, not expansion: the same census
executable, the same taxonomy, the same denominator, the same reporter. The only
new variable is the application.

P1.5's diff-corpus question stays closed. This measures existence, not demand,
exactly as D0.2 did.

## What Wonderous might have been measuring

Wonderous put method tear-offs at 42.72% of marginal unlock and compound writes
at 1.94%. Wonderous is an animation-led showcase app whose blocked methods are
overwhelmingly `build` and `_buildX`, which is precisely where
`onPressed: _handleTap` lives. So the open question is whether that finding is
about **Flutter** or about **this kind of Flutter app**.

The replication corpus is therefore chosen to be structurally *unlike* it:
business/state/data logic, not another showcase.

## Selection rule, precommitted

A candidate qualifies when all five hold:

1. a real application people use, not a sample, template, or gallery;
2. a substantial Dart codebase — on the order of Wonderous or larger;
3. Flutter UI **plus** state/business/data layers, so the mix differs from a
   showcase app;
4. it resolves and compiles to kernel on the pinned toolchain **without source
   modification**;
5. it was not chosen because anything is known about its Route B blocker
   distribution — nothing below has been censused, and no candidate's rows exist
   yet.

**Codegen is not a source modification.** Running a project's own declared
`build_runner` step is part of obtaining its sources, in the same way
`pub get` is. Editing its Dart, changing its constraints, or bumping its
dependencies is disqualifying, and would be recorded as a failure rather than
worked around.

## Candidates, in this order

Attempted in order; the first that satisfies (4) wins. A candidate that fails
(4) is **recorded with its failure** and the next is taken. No candidate is
modified into eligibility, and the order is not revisited after seeing results.

    1  immich        github.com/immich-app/immich, mobile/
                     photo backup client: sync engine, local database, REST
                     client, background upload. Data/state heavy, UI secondary.
    2  spotube       github.com/KRTirtho/spotube
                     music client: service layer, providers, persistence.
    3  localsend     github.com/localsend/localsend, app/
                     file transfer utility: networking, discovery, protocol.
    4  appflowy      github.com/AppFlowy-IO/AppFlowy, frontend/appflowy_flutter/
                     editor + document data model. Largest, most likely to have
                     a native or codegen dependency that fails (4).

Each is pinned to the commit its default branch resolves to at clone time, and
that full SHA is recorded in the result — a branch is not provenance.

## Decision gate, precommitted

Ordering matters; the percentages do not have to match.

* **Tear-offs remain among the top few sole blockers** — even falling from 42.72%
  to, say, 15% — then `D-TEAROFF` opens next. The mechanism looks cheap enough
  that it does not need to win absolutely.
* **Tear-offs collapse** (Wonderous 42.72% against roughly 1–3% here) then
  nothing is implemented on this evidence. Two apps disagreeing that sharply
  would say architectural composition dominates the blocker distribution, and
  the question of what "general Dart support" should optimise for is reopened
  rather than answered.
* **`super` wins** — it is still priced separately. A second-corpus win licenses
  a design investigation, not an implementation lane: unlike a tear-off it
  cannot become `self.foo()` without changing dispatch.

## Manual inspection is part of the method, not a garnish

D0.3 changed the ranking twice by reading real bodies: tear-offs were hiding
inside the `this` category, and `super` was double-reporting through a
`ThisExpression` the CFE synthesises inside `SuperMethodInvocation`. Neither was
visible in the histogram.

So representative methods for the top categories **will** be sampled and read
here too, and any category whose label turns out not to mean what it says will
be corrected in the open, as those two were.
