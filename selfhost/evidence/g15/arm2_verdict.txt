Four things follow, and the last is the one that matters:

1. Patch 1 is **Installed**, NOT Bad{BootCrash}. It was never tombstoned.
2. `last_booted_patch: 1` — it booted successfully afterwards.
3. `queued_events: []` — no PatchInstallFailure was queued. A backout would have
   queued one carrying "crash_recovery: patch 1 failed to boot".
4. **`boot_attempt_count` and `last_boot_attempt_patch` EXIST in pointers.json.**
   Those two fields do not exist in any shipped updater before patch 0010. Their
   presence on this device is independent proof that the engine running here carries
   0010 — the schema itself is the receipt.

WHY THIS IS DISCRIMINATING
--------------------------
Without 0010, `detect_boot_crash_on_init` marks Bad{BootCrash} on the FIRST
un-succeeded boot. Tap 1 was exactly that: the patch was active and the process died
before Engine::Run returned, so no success was recorded. Tap 2 would then have shown
OLD-kill against a tombstoned patch.

Tap 2 showed NEW-kill. So the breadcrumb was cleared and the patch RETRIED, which is
the behaviour 0010 adds and nothing else in the system provides.

Taps 3 and 4 repeat it independently, satisfying the design's requirement that arm 2
run more than once — one survival is consistent with the kill having landed outside
the window by luck; two is not.

WHAT WAS NOT DIRECTLY OBSERVED, and it matters
-----------------------------------------------
`boot_attempt_count` was read as **0**, not 1. By the time the state was pulled, a
successful boot (tap 4) had already reset it — which is `record_boot_success` doing
its job. So the counter was never caught mid-arm at 1.

The pass therefore rests on the OUTCOME (patch Installed, booted, no failure event,
NEW-kill on screen twice after kills) rather than on catching the counter in flight.
That is sufficient for the claim being made — the patch was not tombstoned — but it
is not a direct observation of the threshold arithmetic, and a future run wanting
that should pull pointers.json BETWEEN a kill and the next launch.

The screenshot at /tmp/ks_arm2_F.png is white and does NOT show tap 4: pulling the
state files relaunched the app after the taps. The screen observation is the
operator's report; the state files are the corroboration.

WHY BY HAND
-----------
There is no automated launch path here. `ios-deploy --justlaunch` quits the app when
lldb detaches, and an attached `--debug` launch holds it at the launch screen with the
debugger intercepting signals — unusable for an arm whose mechanism IS a signal. A
positive control established this rather than assumption: airgap_probe, PROVEN on
device with release 39, showed the same "no UI" under the same automation, which is
what stopped a false report that patch 0009 had broken app startup.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM
------------------------
* NOT crash-backout. A patch that genuinely breaks Dart reaching the threshold and
  being marked Bad is the COMPLEMENTARY arm and was not run. G15's §15 gate "a
  Dart-phase crash backs the patch out" is NOT closed by this.
* NOT restart-required (§8).
* NOT that 0009 alone is safe. 0009 and 0010 shipped in one cell and were tested
  together; this says the PAIR does not tombstone a good patch.
