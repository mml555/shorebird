## Item 5, precisely

The fix is real and load-bearing: the **Flutter SDK group now completes from an
empty cache**, where before it could not get past `sky_engine`. The build then
fails one group later:

    [1/1] iOS SDK
      ├─ [1/3] ios
    Failed to download …/3b2471e8…/ios/artifacts.zip → 404

`flutter_cache.dart`'s `_iosBinaryDirs` requires all three iOS variants —
`ios`, `ios-profile`, `ios-release` — unconditionally, even for a release build.
This engine tree contains only `out/ios_release`: the debug and profile iOS
engines were never built, so those two artifacts do not exist to publish.

**Borrowing them was refused for the same reason as `sky_engine`.** They would
come from a different engine build. Their impact is materially smaller — debug
and profile iOS artifacts do not participate in a release build's output — but
"smaller" is not "provenance-closed", and the point of this exercise was to stop
depending on artifacts nobody had bound.

Measured across the chain with `verify_cell_completeness.sh`:

    2c4443ce   INCOMPLETE — 4 of 9 absent
    93a3756    INCOMPLETE — 4 of 9 absent
    ac5d3b63   INCOMPLETE — 4 of 9 absent
    9b5f040c   INCOMPLETE — 4 of 9 absent
    3b2471e8   INCOMPLETE — 2 of 9 absent   <- this epoch

So the remaining cost is concrete: **build the `ios_debug` and `ios_profile`
engine variants**, package their `artifacts.zip`, and mint again. Until then no
cell in this fork can build from a genuinely empty cache, and that is now
measured rather than assumed.

## The invariant is enforced, not just written down

`verify_cell_completeness.sh` derives the required set from the **consumer's own
inventory** — `flutter_cache.dart`'s `getPackageDirs()` and the binary-dir tables
— rather than from a list kept here. A list kept here is exactly how
`sky_engine.zip` went missing from four published cells while
`mint_route_b_cell.sh`'s header claimed it was cloned.

`mint_route_b_cell.sh` now runs it before publishing and **refuses an incomplete
cell**, with `ALLOW_INCOMPLETE_CELL=1` as an explicit, noisy override. Confirmed
by re-running the mint: it refused.

**A first version of the checker over-collected** — it grepped every
`<String>[…]` pair in the consumer and demanded 33 artifacts including Android
and darwin-x64 desktop, reporting a usable cell as missing 27 things. Recorded
because over-collecting is as wrong as hardcoding: a check that cries wolf gets
silenced. It is now scoped to what an iOS release on macOS arm64 actually
activates, and a derivation that yields nothing is a hard error rather than a
vacuous pass.
