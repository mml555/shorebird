## Why this is the piece it is

The two holes share one property that makes them one unit: the analyzer **accepts** and the failure lands downstream wearing a lowering bug's clothes. They are BUGS, not GAPS, and the classification rule applies in both directions — source already determines both behaviors, so **neither is a device question** and neither may book `R1`.

It deliberately does **not** include: any new private *capability* (P2 is decided, `PARITY.md:845` and `:981`); re-opening the threat model (`PARITY.md:592`, decided, with the expiry condition at `:1047`); a private *write* or a private *method call taking arguments* (both still unclaimed on device, `PARITY.md:362`, `evidence/releases/32/RECORDED`); and the third, verdict-level hole (`PARITY.md:1970-1974`) unless the open question below is answered yes.

The split matters more than the fixes, and the split has changed since this order was first drafted. **Group A is CLI-only and pays nothing** — that half is unaffected and runnable now. **Group B's discount is gone.** Queue item 6 asked for a fold-in rather than paying twice; the mint it meant to fold into is spent, so the honest reading is that group B *missed the window* and should now wait for the next `R3` build that happens for another reason. Landing it anyway is not merely expensive — see the skew hazard on step 5, which breaks every consumer of the published cell.
